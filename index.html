01

one sentence, not one script

create-a-checkapi.runbrowser.dev/v1/checks
// No selectors. No runner. No CI minutes.
// Say what should be true, and how often to look.

{
  "name": "Checkout reaches the payment step",
  "task": "Go to example.com, add the first product to the
           basket, open checkout, and confirm the page
           shows a total and a card payment option.",
  "intervalMinutes": 15
}

The first run works out how — which links, which buttons, which words prove it worked. Every run after that repeats those steps with no language model involved, which is why one check every fifteen minutes is roughly three thousand runs a month and still costs less than the coffee you drank writing this sentence. 02

what makes it hold up

It repairs itself. It never covers for you.

Nine times out of ten a broken test means somebody renamed a div. We notice that a step can no longer be carried out, work the journey out again, and carry on — you never hear about it.

But when every step still works and the page simply says something else, we change nothing and tell you. That is the whole discipline. A check that quietly adapted to the new number would go green over your regression, and you would believe it, because it is a check.

A failure that tells you what happened

Not "red". Which assertion, what it expected, and the words that are on the page instead — with the URL it read them from and a replay of the browser getting there. Enough to know whether to page someone before you open a laptop.

Watch the one that failed

Every run happens in a real browser you can watch live, and a failed one can be replayed frame by frame afterwards — for the 3am break with nobody at a screen.

Behind the login

Most of what matters is past a sign-in. Do it once, keep the session, and every check after that starts already authenticated — no credentials in a task, no login on every run.

From where your customers are

Run a check from Germany and the browser's clock, language and IP all agree. The bugs that only happen in one country are the ones nobody finds from the office.

No model in the loop

A language model works the journey out once. After that it is gone, so a run is deterministic and costs browser-seconds. That is why hourly checks are priced like a rounding error.

EU, always

Browsers, screenshots and results stay inside the European Union. If your checks run against a product handling personal data, that is one conversation you do not have to have.

A browser platform underneath

Checks sit on top of hosted Chromium you can also drive yourself over plain CDP — Playwright, Puppeteer, whatever you already have. It is there when you need it, and there is nothing to learn when you don't.

So it catchesYour site being broken nowYour branch being broken then
InfrastructureNoneRunners, browsers, minutes, a queue

Not a replacement for unit tests, and not really a replacement for a CI suite either — those tell you a change is bad before it ships. This tells you the live site is broken now, which is the failure your customers meet and the one a suite on a push cannot see.

Check runs are the number to look at, and they are large because a run has no language model in it — working out how to do something costs tokens, repeating it costs browser-seconds. The allowance is sized by one rule: every check your tier allows can run hourly. It is a pool, so spend it differently if you like — five checks every fifteen minutes costs the same as twenty hourly ones. Runs are cheap to give because a run has no language model in it; re-deriving a journey after a redesign does, so those are allowanced separately (10 / 60 / 400 / 2,000 a month) at a level a real suite never reaches. Going over does not stop your checks — monitoring that goes quiet when you grow goes quiet exactly when you need it. Extra runs are €0.01 and extra re-derivations €0.05, both priced above what a plan works out at, so moving up a tier is always cheaper than paying overage on the one below. Overage is capped at your own plan price by default, so it can never more than double a bill — set it to zero and it is refused entirely. Every plan — the free one included — gets the live viewer, evidence on every failure, saved login sessions, geo-matched checks and real Chrome with a coherent device profile. Recorded replays are Pro and up: the one thing tied to a tier rather than sized by an allowance, because storing the frames costs us money on every recorded run and we would rather say so than let you discover it. Underneath, every plan carries the browser platform itself — hosted Chromium over plain CDP, plus /fetch, /search and /extract — billed per second, floored at 10s.
